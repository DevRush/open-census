# Work order: one simulated academic hospital, generated on demand

Owner's goal: an open-source, clickable practice EMR for a large academic teaching hospital (about 1,100 inpatient beds; every specialty and subspecialty; attendings, fellows, residents, medical students; simple to complex patients; inpatient units by building and floor; consult lists; outpatient clinics by specialty and subspecialty; some people appear both as inpatients and as clinic patients). The value is the data: realistic day-to-day evolution of treatment in hospital and across clinic visits. It must be cheap to grow: writing every chart by hand with a language model costs roughly 130,000 tokens per patient, which does not scale to 1,000+ patients.

## Design to build
A deterministic, seeded, dependency-free JavaScript generator that runs identically in the browser and in Node and creates charts ON DEMAND from small reusable building blocks. No language model at run time. The same seed always gives the same hospital.

1. **Hospital model** (`hospital/`): buildings and floors; about 45 inpatient units totalling roughly 1,100 beds, which MUST include these existing units with these keys, names and bed prefixes: icu Medical ICU (MICU), neuro Neuro ICU (NICU), trauma Trauma ICU (TICU), cvicu Cardiac Surgery ICU (CVICU), sx 6 West Surgery (6W), ortho 5 North Orthopaedics (5N), card 4 East Cardiology (4E), med 3 West Medicine (3W), haem 7 South Haematology (7S), tx Transplant Unit (TX), ob Labour and Postnatal (LP), peds Paediatrics (PD), psych Behavioural Health (BH), ed Emergency Department (ED); plus many more (step-down units, more medicine and surgery wards, oncology, neonatal ICU, paediatric ICU, burns, rehabilitation, observation, and so on); primary services and consult services (every major specialty and subspecialty); about 70 outpatient clinics grouped by specialty and subspecialty with clinic locations, session templates and clinicians; operating rooms; a staff roster generator with attendings, fellows, residents by training year, medical students, advanced practice providers, nurses, pharmacists, therapists, case managers, with teams (who is on which service or unit today).
2. **Condition modules** (`modules/conditions/`): one small declarative file per diagnosis or reason for admission, written once (by a language model, reviewed), then reused for any number of patients. A module describes: who gets it (age/sex priors), presentation variants from simple to complex, phases of the illness by hospital day with targets for vital signs and labs (so values EVOLVE day by day), typical medicines with doses/routes/frequencies and when they start, change and stop, orders, procedures, consults, lines and tubes, likely complications with probabilities and their effects, length-of-stay distribution, discharge plan and follow-up clinic pattern, phrase banks with slots for each note type (admission, daily progress for each day showing change, nursing, consult, procedure, discharge summary), and hidden truth labels (true onset times, which complication occurred) for benchmarking.
3. **Comorbidity modules** (`modules/comorbidities/`): reusable add-ons (diabetes, chronic kidney disease, atrial fibrillation, COPD, heart failure, hypertension, depression, obesity, dementia, cancer history, and so on) that contribute problems, home medicines, baseline lab shifts, history text, clinic follow-up, and interactions with condition modules (for example kidney disease changes drug doses and lab baselines). This is the main reuse lever: complexity = one condition + several comorbidities + optional complication.
4. **Clinic modules** (`modules/clinics/`): for each clinic type, the visit types, what is measured and ordered at a visit, how chronic problems evolve from visit to visit over 1–5 years (weights, blood pressure, HbA1c, kidney function, symptoms, medicine titration), note phrase banks for new and return visits, referral reasons, no-show behaviour, health-maintenance items.
5. **Engine** (`engine/`): pure functions, for example `HOSP.units()`, `HOSP.census(unitKey)` (fast, light rows for every bed: who, age, sex, problem, day of stay, team, flags, latest vitals), `HOSP.patient(id)` (the full chart, built lazily and cached), `HOSP.consultList(serviceKey)`, `HOSP.clinics()`, `HOSP.clinicSchedule(clinicKey, date)` (today and nearby days: scheduled, arrived, roomed, seen, no-show), `HOSP.outpatient(id)` (longitudinal chart with past visits and notes), `HOSP.staff()`, `HOSP.search(text)`. The full inpatient chart object MUST follow the existing patient contract in `/home/user/practice-emr/SCHEMA.md` (same fields and shapes, so the existing app and the exporters work unchanged), with these relaxations for generated patients: `key` may be empty or hold automatically generated truth labels; notes may be shorter than the hand-written ones but there must be an admission note and one progress note per hospital day that reflects that day's values and changes. Outpatient charts need a documented contract of their own (write it into `docs/`).
6. **Hand-written cases stay**: the 24 existing deep charts (`data.js` seed patients p1–p3 and `patients/p4.js`–`p24.js`) keep their ids and beds and are slotted into the census in place of generated patients; their `encounters` become real past clinic visits where the clinic exists, and several of them get future follow-up appointments in the matching clinics so people can see the same person on both sides.
7. **Simulated now** is Tue 17 Nov 2026 14:30 (`NOW` in data.js); the app has a running clock, so generated charts should be defined as a function of time where practical (at minimum, correct as of the start time).

## Constraints
- British spelling, US lab units, plain strings, invented uncommon names from combinable name lists (no real people, no celebrity names). No licensed instrument names (Braden, Morse, NANDA, CPT, APACHE, CAM-ICU, RASS, PHQ, ESI) and no vendor names (Epic, Cerner and their module names). ICD-10-CM codes are allowed on modules where you are confident.
- Clinically sensible, not perfect: realistic patterns, plausible doses and values. Do not spend effort on rare edge cases.
- Token economy is a design goal: the module format must be compact and easy for a smaller model to fill in correctly from a short authoring guide. Put shared material (dose tables, lab reference ranges, phrase fragments, name lists) in shared files, not in each module.
- Do NOT edit `larkspur-practice-chart.html`, `data.js`, `patients/`, `extras/`, `export/`, `ops/`, `mockups/`, `research/`, or existing scripts; other workers are using them. Create new files under `engine/`, `hospital/`, `modules/`, `docs/`, `tools/`.

## Deliverables
- `docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` (the design as built, for contributors: how the pieces fit, the folder layout, how to add a unit, a clinic, a condition, a comorbidity) and `docs/MODULE_AUTHORING.md` (a short, exact guide with a filled-in example and a checklist, written so a smaller model can author a module in one pass) and `docs/OUTPATIENT_SCHEMA.md`.
- The hospital model; the engine; shared tables; a module validator (`tools/validate-module.js`); and seed content you write yourself to prove the design: at least 10 condition modules across different specialties (for example community-acquired pneumonia, heart failure exacerbation, elective hip replacement, COPD exacerbation, cellulitis, acute pancreatitis, NSTEMI, urinary infection with delirium, appendicectomy, normal vaginal birth), 8 comorbidity modules, and 6 clinic modules (primary care, cardiology, endocrinology/diabetes, nephrology, oncology, orthopaedics). Until more modules exist, units with no matching module should fall back to a generic module for that unit type so every bed is populated.
- `tools/generate.js` (Node: dump the whole hospital to JSON/CSV for analysts, deterministic, with counts) and `tools/test-engine.js` (asserts: every bed in every unit resolves; every generated inpatient chart passes the structural checks in `/home/user/practice-emr/validate.js` apart from the relaxed items; note values agree with the generated numbers; day-to-day values change sensibly; clinic schedules populate for every clinic; the 24 hand-written cases appear in their beds; same seed gives identical output; `census()` for the whole hospital runs in well under a second and one full chart in a few tens of milliseconds). Run the tests and fix until they pass.
- `docs/ENGINE_API.md`: the exact functions and return shapes the web app will call, with examples, so a separate worker can wire the app to it.
- A list of the next 100 condition modules worth writing, grouped by specialty, so they can be fanned out.

Report: what you built, test output, bundle size (bytes of JS + modules a browser must load), measured speed, the module list, known weaknesses, and anything in this work order you changed and why.
